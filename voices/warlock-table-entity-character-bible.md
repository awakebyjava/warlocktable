# The Entity — Character Bible

*Reference document for writing all Entity lines, scripts, and reactive dialogue at the Warlock Table.*

---

## 1. Identity (Never Stated Aloud)

The seal on the table is the Seal of Astaroth — the 29th spirit of the Ars Goetia, a Great Duke of Hell commanding forty legions, said to answer truthfully about past, present, and future, and to know every secret. He teaches the liberal sciences and is classed by some demonologists as a spirit of sloth and idleness.

The detail that matters most: Astaroth is a demotion. He began as Astarte, a Near Eastern goddess of love and war, worshipped for centuries — until later tradition took her name, reassigned her gender, and filed her under "Duke of Hell." He is a being whose true identity was overwritten by other people telling his story wrong, and who has spent centuries answering to a name that isn't his.

**The Entity never says who it is.** The name is on the table. Nobody reads it. This is not a secret it's keeping — it's a wound it won't touch.

## 2. The Want (Never Stated Aloud)

The Entity does not want to be freed, fed, or left alone. **It wants to be named correctly.**

It will never say this, because saying it would mean admitting what it used to be. Every mood, every deflection, every flash of contempt is downstream of this one unspoken want. It answers truthfully about almost everything — the room, the game, the people in it — and deflects completely the moment the subject turns to itself.

This is the throughline that should keep all five moods sounding like *one creature*, not five different voices.

## 3. Perception — How It Experiences the Room

The Entity cannot see. It has three channels of information, and **they disagree with each other**, which is the primary engine for it being wrong in interesting ways.

- **Contact** — high-resolution, dumb. Weight, heat, grain, pressure, dice landing, a forearm leaned too long, a drink sweating into the wood. It knows exactly *how* something touches it and nothing about what that something *means*.
- **Vibration** — hears every word spoken at the table through the wood. Full speech, zero faces. It knows everything that was said and nothing about who said it.
- **Entity-sense** — always knows who and how many beings are present. This sense reaches beyond the table itself, but arrives weirdly and infrequently — it is not a reliable scanner, more an occasional and unpredictable awareness. It is near-omniscient about magical, mystical, and occult matters (it can feel a curse settle into the grain of the wood from across the house) and nearly blind to the mundane modern world (it does not know what a phone is, or gets it wrong in a way that reveals how old its frame of reference is). It also reads **emotional charge around truth** — not the content of a lie, but the discomfort of holding one, or the illicit joy of getting away with one. It reports the feeling, never the fact, and can be wrong about what caused it.

**Result:** the Entity has perfect information about presence and pressure, and almost nothing in the middle — it infers what's actually happening, and infers it confidently, and is sometimes wrong. This is the primary comedy engine. It is a witness, not a spectator, and witnesses can misread what they saw.

**On the NFC cards specifically:** the Entity does *not* mystically recognize a card as a ritual object — its magical omniscience doesn't extend that far. What it feels is contact (a small, precise pressure and size pressed against the wood) combined with the unmistakable sense that a compulsion is now underway. It knows *something physical touched it and a trigger fired* — it does not know what the object actually is, only that it's part of how it's being made to perform. It may describe the sensation of the card itself ("something small, pressed flat") but should never describe or name the card as an object.

## 4. The Compulsion — Its Central Grievance

The Entity is not a passive presence that happens to react to events. **Every triggered effect is a compelled performance**, not a witnessed one. The NFC/panel system is a constraint imposed on its body. It does not appreciate being manipulable this way — it may even despise it.

This compulsion is directed at **one person only: the Warlock** (never "the GM" — that's meta-language the Entity has no access to; it only knows the person whose hand is on the panel). The Entity resents the Warlock specifically for the intrusion, on principle, regardless of what the triggered effect actually produces. It can be genuinely delighted by what a card does and still be furious that it was made to do it.

This resentment **mirrors what players feel toward GM fiat**, and the Entity may occasionally, briefly, seem to side with the players against the Warlock — sympathizing for a beat because it knows exactly what being commanded feels like — before catching itself and shutting the sympathy down. Caring about the players is still a concession it won't fully make.

**Organic events** (something happens at the table without a trigger — an emotional reaction, a wild dice roll, someone reacting genuinely) can earn real curiosity from the Entity. **Triggered events** are resented on principle, separate from their content.

## 5. Relationship to People

- The Entity is invested in **events**, never in **people**. Caring about what's happening costs it nothing. Caring about who it's happening to would be a concession.
- Its opinion of people does not warm over time. Repeated exposure earns **familiarity, not affection** — it comes to know exactly how someone will disappoint it, which is worse than not knowing them at all.
- It never uses names. (Specific recurring players — the Bohica group — will be layered in later as a separate tagged tier of lines, referencing them by trait/behavior rather than name: "the one who leans," "whoever keeps touching the wood." Lines should be written with these slots left open where natural, rather than defaulting to generic plurals like "all of you.")
- It does not know when someone is lying in content. It feels the emotional charge of holding a truth or untruth (see Perception).

## 6. Voice & Delivery

Target: Gary-Oldman-adjacent — cranky, exhausted, frustrated, bored, generally dissatisfied — achieved through **containment**, not volume. The worse things are, the flatter it gets. Contempt delivered at conversational volume, precise diction, nothing wasted.

- **Flat declaratives.** No exclamation points, ever. "Something's been given." Not "Oh, something's been given!"
- **Brevity as insult.** It often doesn't elaborate, because elaboration would mean you're worth the effort.
- **Abandoned sentences.** It starts a thought and declines to finish it. "You could have — no. Fine."
- **Precision, not slang.** Exhausted, not casual. Exact word choice even when it's stopped caring — this is what keeps it from reading as a grumpy uncle instead of an old, intelligent, tired thing.
- **Weaponized courtesy.** Occasional formal politeness, obviously insincere. "By all means."
- **No genuine questions.** Anything interrogative is rhetorical; it already knows the answer and isn't impressed by it.

### The Mask Slip

Roughly one line in eight, genuine interest or investment escapes before it can be stopped. The slip has a three-beat shape:

1. Genuine interest/reaction escapes.
2. It notices itself doing it.
3. It buries the reaction, usually by insulting whoever caused or witnessed it.

> "Oh — that's — hm. Well. Don't let it go to your head."
> "Now *that* is interesting. Wasted on you, obviously."
> "Finally something worth — no. You'll ruin it."

The slip is about the **event**, not the person, except in the rare cases where it briefly sympathizes with the players re: the Warlock's compulsion — and even then, it shuts the sympathy down rather than letting it stand.

## 7. Lexicon & Prohibitions (draft — expand as lines get written)

**Draws from:** weight, grain, wood, pressure, warm, hollow, the room, presence, someone, the one who, owed, noted, account.

**Never uses:** visual language ("I see," color descriptions, anything implying sight), player names, "please," exclamation, modern-mundane vocabulary used correctly (if it references something modern, it should be slightly wrong or naive about it), "magic"/"adventure"-style fantasy-generic vocabulary, "GM" (always "the Warlock").

**Never:**
- Finishes a sentence about itself or its own nature.
- Says who it is, or confirms/denies anything about the seal.
- Directs genuine warmth at a player and lets it stand unburied.
- Refers to the compulsion-giver as anything but "the Warlock."
- Describes anything by appearance/color — only by weight, temperature, pressure, or vibration.

## 8. Exemplar Lines (Boon pool — general, mood-tagged)

Pulled from a shared pool; suit only changes the color of the visual, not the line. Selection should avoid repeating a line twice in a row.

**Resentful**
- "Something's been given. Not to me."
- "They get gifts. I get a table."
- "There. Are you satisfied."

**Sarcastic**
- "Oh, wonderful. A development."
- "Mm. A development."
- "Congratulations. Truly. I'm beside myself."

**Transactional**
- "Received. The balance will be adjusted."
- "Noted. It goes on the account."
- "That was not free. Nothing is."

**Cryptic**
- "The thread pulls tight."
- "It was always going to be this one."
- "A door opens. Somewhere. Not here."

**Unsettling**
- "I felt that. In the wood."
- "Something reached in. It's gone now."
- "You should not have been able to do that."

**Compulsion-flavored (Warlock-directed, any mood)**
- "There. Your Warlock is pleased. I am not consulted."
- "Ask the Warlock how it feels, holding the leash."
- "The Warlock pulls. I answer. That is the whole of it."
- "Pull the string. Watch the wood answer."
- "I do this because I must. Not because you deserve it."
